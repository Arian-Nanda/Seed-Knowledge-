The '''player''' is a unique [[entity]] or [[mob]]{{only|be|short=1}} whose actions are [[control]]led by the real person, and not the game itself. They are the main character of singleplayer ''Minecraft'' [[world]]s, where most interactions and [[mechanics]] revolve around them as part of the [[gameplay]]. Players may also partake in the [[multiplayer]] mode with other players, putting more emphasis on player-driven activities.

== Appearance ==
{{Anchor|Skin|Model}}

Every player's model uses a [[skin]] as its texture, composed of one full texture file or multiple [[Character Creator]] items.{{only|be|short=1}} Players can choose a skin to use prior to launch in the ''[[Minecraft Launcher]]''{{only|je|short=1}} or at any time in-game in the [[Dressing Room]].{{only|be|short=1}}

Nine default skins are always available: [[Steve]], [[Alex]], [[Zuri]], [[Sunny]], [[Noor]], [[Makena]], [[Kai]], [[Efe]], or [[Ari]]. Players can obtain custom skins from [[skin packs]] or other content in the [[Marketplace]], third-party resources, or create their own. Classic skins can have either "classic" or "slim" shapes, which makes their arm models wide or slim. External skin textures cannot be imported on consoles.
[[File:Heights.png|thumb|Different character heights]]
{{IN|BE}}, the [[Character Creator]] allows players to compose a character from multiple persona items, some of which are animated or use custom geometry, obtained from completing [[achievements]], various events, or [[Minecoins]]. These characters can be further customized with four different player heights and slim or wide arms.

A [[cape]] can be added to any skin, which is a cosmetic item obtained from special events. Characters from the Character Creator periodically blink their eyes in ''Bedrock Edition''.

In ''Bedrock Edition'', any character can also be assigned a set of 4 [[emotes]], obtained similarly to character pieces, which they can express in-game by pressing {{Keys|B}}.

Characters and classic skins are synchronized with the player's Microsoft account and available on any device, except imported skin files in ''Bedrock Edition''. {{IN|JE}}, playing in the [[Demo mode|demo]] or offline does not allow usage of custom skins and always applies a random default skin.

The player's appearance can be obscured by equipping [[armor]], such as a [[turtle shell]], [[helmet]]s, [[chestplate]]s, [[legging]]s, and [[boot]]s, as well as other equippable items such as an [[elytra]], a [[carved pumpkin]], and [[head]]s of mobs and other players.

In ''Java Edition'', if the player is shot with an [[arrow]] or stung by a [[bee]], the arrow or stinger appears physically embedded inside the player for a given time. These do not apply to other mobs.

== Drops ==
*The content of their [[inventory]] (except any items with [[Curse of Vanishing]])
*Current XP level multiplied by 7, which is then the amount of points dropped. This is capped at 100 XP points, which corresponds to just over level 7. 

If the game rule "Keep inventory" (or {{Game rule link|keep_inventory}}) is enabled, nothing is dropped.

== Health and hunger meters ==
The player has 10 full hearts or 20 [[health points]], visible on the [[HUD]]. Players in Survival mode can take [[damage]], depleting health points and eventually [[killing]] the player. After death, players respawn at their spawn point, which can be a [[bed]] or [[Respawn Anchor|respawn anchor]], or the [[world spawn]] if absent.

When the player's [[hunger]] bar is at {{Hunger|18}} or higher and "Natural regeneration" is enabled, health slowly regenerates by {{Hp|1}} every four seconds, but when it is at {{Hunger|20}} with saturation remaining, health regenerates by {{Hp|1}} every half second.{{Only|Java|short=JE}} The hunger bar depletes faster from energy-intensive activities like [[sprinting]], [[jumping]], attacking [[mobs]], and [[mining]], and it can be refilled by eating [[food]]. If the hunger bar is at {{Hunger|17}} or lower, the player does not regenerate health. While the hunger bar is at {{Hunger|0}}, the player is starving and proceeds to lose health. On Hard [[difficulty]], starvation kills the player, while on Normal or Easy, it depletes health to {{hp|1}} or {{hp|10}}, respecitvely. 

On Peaceful difficulty, the hunger bar does not deplete and the player can always regenerate health.

Like any other mob, players can posess [[status effects]] that positively, neutrally, or negatively affect them.

== Movement ==
{{see also|Controls}}

=== Walking ===

The most basic movement the player can perform is [[walking]]. The player can walk at a reasonable speed rate of 4.317 [[Blocks tall|blocks]] per second{{fn|About 15.5 kph or 9.7 mph, which is a 3:51 kilometer or a 6:12 mile.}}, all while taking no [[hunger]] impact. This means that the player can walk a total of 5181 blocks{{fn|About 5.2 kilometer.}} in [[Daylight cycle|one ''Minecraft'' day]], in a straight line, without sprinting or sneaking.

=== Sprinting ===
The player can move faster by [[sprinting]], but at the cost of draining the [[hunger]] bar considerably while doing so. The player sprints approximately 5.612 [[Blocks tall|blocks]] per second{{fn|About 20.2 kph or 12.6&nbsp;mph, which is a 2:58&nbsp;km or a 4:46 mile.}}, as opposed to the regular pace of 4.317 blocks per second. The maximum distance a player can jump when sprinting is approximately<!-- 4.225 value is from JUST running until you get to max speed, and then doing a single jump. to a couple more decimal points, value is 4.224812.

5 blocks is NOT possible without some major caveats --> 4.225 blocks. The [[Speed]] [[effect]] increases momentum. The player cannot sprint if the hunger bar is at {{hunger|6}} or less. Sprinting is activated by double-tapping the forward key (default {{key|W}}), then holding it, or by holding the sprint key ({{key|Control}} by default, {{key|Command}} if using a Mac) while pressing the forward key. Holding the sprint key in [[Creative mode]] while [[flying]] causes the player to fly faster.

=== Sneaking ===
[[Sneaking]] is a feature activated by pressing and holding the sneak key (default is {{key|Shift}}). Sneaking prevents players from falling more than half a block, making it highly useful for building horizontally outward over space. Going past the edge of a block and stopping sneaking does not result in falling off that block. The player can still dismount blocks while sneaking by jumping over the block's edge. In [[multiplayer]], a player's name tag is grayed out in ''Java Edition'' or completely disappears in Bedrock Edition when they sneak, to relate to the fact that sneaking makes them harder to see.

=== Jumping ===
The maximum height a player can [[Jumping|jump]] without the [[Jump Boost]] effect is about 1.2522 blocks in Java Edition. The jump height on Bedrock Edition is shorter (similar to the pre-[[Combat Update]]) with the value of 1.24919 blocks.

=== Swimming ===
[[Swimming]] occurs when the player is sprinting (by double pressing {{Keys|W}}, or pressing {{Keys|Control}} or {{Keys|Command}}) while the player is submerged underwater. Swimming has the same animation as crawling.

=== Crawling ===
[[Crawling]] occurs when the player is in an area less than 1.5 blocks high and prevents suffocation.

=== Climbing ===
Climbing occurs whenever the player's lower half is in the block occupied by a climbable block, such as a [[ladder]] or [[vines]]. If this condition is satisfied:

* If the player pushes against a wall, the player moves upward at about 2.35 blocks per second instead of horizontally. (Thus inside a 1×1 ladder shaft, the player can use any movement key to move upward.)
* The player can ascend the ladder by holding jump instead of using a horizontal movement key.
* If a player moving perpendicular to the climbable block enters said block, the player begins to move upward, but then falls off the block shortly afterward if the player continues to move horizontally.
* The player's maximum downward speed is reduced to a "descending" speed, at about 3 blocks per second.
* The player's horizontal movement friction is the same as on [[ice]]. (That is, moving around in the tiny space the player is in while still hanging on the ladder at the same level.)

Additionally:

* In ''Bedrock Edition'', a player can hold down the jump button while climbing to make the climbing speed faster.
* A player on a [[slab]] in front of the climbable block must jump to get on despite being only half a block down.
* Consuming [[food]], blocking with a [[shield]] or pulling back a [[bow]], do not slow the player down while climbing (unlike when [[walking]], [[sprinting]] and [[swimming]]).

== Movement speed ==

There isn't a simple hard-coded maximum velocity for walking, sprinting, etc. The maximum is a result of a base acceleration countered by "friction" or air resistance. The base acceleration for walking is 0.098, and sprinting is 30% faster than that. Velocity is increased by this acceleration value every tick (1/20th of a second), then the player is moved by the resulting velocity, then the velocity is multiplied by the friction value of the block they are walking on. For most blocks, the friction value is 0.546. As the player walks forward, each tick their velocity goes +0.098, *0.546, +.098, *0.546, +0.098, and so on. The result is that eventually, the 0.546 multiplier counteracts the 0.098 increase, so the player doesn't go any faster. The formula for this "terminal velocity" is a/(1-r), where a is the acceleration, and r is the block's friction. 0.098/(1-0.546) ≈ 0.2159 meters/tick = 4.317 meters/second. Sprinting is 30% faster than that, at about 5.612 meters/second. Flying is similar, but the base acceleration is 0.049 and the "friction" is 0.91, which comes out to ~10.89&nbsp;m/s.

== Gameplay HUD ==
The onscreen [[heads-up display]] (HUD) consists of the player's [[health]] bar, [[hunger]] bar, [[experience]] bar, and [[hotbar]] among with many other UI elements shown sporadically.

The [[armor]] rating bar appears above the health bar if the player is wearing armor and the oxygen bar appears if the player is submerged in [[water]] or is suffocating in a block.{{only|be|short=1}} The HUD also contains the crosshair and a held object or fist. Appearance of HUD elements be toggled with {{key|F1}}, in the [[settings]], or using {{cmd|hud}}.

When the player is in [[Creative]] mode, the health, hunger, experience, armor, and oxygen bars are hidden, and only the hotbar is visible. Despite this, the player can still collect experience and wear armor. In [[Spectator]] mode, the hotbar, crosshair, and held object/fist are also hidden; {{in|je}}, the hotbar appears as a player spectating list when a number is pressed or the {{mouse|scroll}} is moved.

== Experience ==
{{Main|Experience}}
Experience points (XP) can be gained via [[experience orbs]] when killing mobs or mining certain minerals. The current level is indicated by a green number above the HUD, and the experience points can be used to enchant weapons, tools, or armor with different useful attributes and skills (see [[enchanting table]].) [[Anvil]]s require experience to use.

The level increases by obtaining enough experience points. All levels and points are lost upon death but can be partially restored by picking up the experience orbs at the place of death.<ref>http://www.pcgamer.com/first-details-on-minecrafts-xp-system-notch-when-you-die-you-lose-all-levels-you-lose-all-xp/</ref>

Experience is also obtained through activities such as [[Breaking|mining]], [[fishing]], mob [[breeding]], killing certain [[mob]]s, [[trading]], or [[smelting]].

== Game modes ==
{{Main|Game mode}}
* In [[Survival]] mode, the player can place and destroy most blocks, and use all tools available. The player has limited health ({{health|2|notag=1}} icons), hunger ({{hunger|2|notag=1}} icons), and oxygen. When the player runs out of health, they [[die]] and respawn at their spawn point.
** In [[Hardcore]] mode, the player can respawn only in [[Spectator]] mode, and the [[difficulty]] level is locked on Hard mode.
* In [[Creative]] mode, the player can [[fly]] by double-tapping the jump key and have access to infinite items from the [[Creative inventory]]. No mobs attack the player, and the player cannot take damage and die (except in the [[void]] and using {{cmd|kill}} in ''Java Edition''). The player also has no hunger, unlimited oxygen, and can [[break]] blocks instantly.
* In [[Adventure]] mode, there are no changes from Survival mode aside from being unable to break or place blocks unless they possess a tool with the <code>[[Data component format#can_break|can_break]]</code> item component for that block, or have a block with the <code>[[Data component format#can_place_on|can_place_on]]</code> component. This game mode is used for [[World (downloadable content)|adventure maps]].
* [[Spectator]] mode is similar to Creative mode, but the player cannot interact with the world in any way. Players in Spectator are invisible to players in other game modes, are always flying, and can phase through blocks. In ''Java Edition'', the player can also spectate mobs and inspect inventories.

== Username ==
Players in-game are referred to by a username. These are used to target the player with [[commands]], play online [[multiplayer]], and differentiate other players.  

{{IN|je}}, usernames must be 3-16 characters, although there are exceptions for players with under 3 characters, who bought the game early in its development. Players can change their username no more than once every 30 days on the [https://minecraft.net/profile preferences page] of minecraft.net. When the player changes their username, the previous username becomes available for other users to claim after 37 days. Because players can change usernames every 30 days, a player can manage two usernames without anyone being able to take either of them. If the player has a username under 3 characters and changes it, the old sub-3-character username is permanently unable to be obtained again.<ref>https://namemc.com/name/NY</ref> This also applies for symbol names.<ref>https://namemc.com/search?q=y-93</ref>

{{IN|be}}, the players username is the same as the Xbox {{w|Gamertag}} of the player's Microsoft account. Gamertags can be changed on the [https://social.xbox.com/changegamertag change gamertag page] in the Xbox website for a fee of {{w|US$}}9.99, though changing it for the first time is free.<ref>https://support.xbox.com/help/account-profile/profile/change-xbox-live-gamertag</ref> However, players can sign out of their Microsoft account and change the username in-game to any of 3-32 characters long in the Profile [[settings]]. While signed out, multiplayer is inaccessible. Changing the player's name to "Dinnerbone" or "Grumm" turns the player model upside-down.

Player names appear above their head as nameplates, typically in white letters within a dark transparent rectangle. Player nameplates can also be seen through solid blocks and other obstructions, although a player can [[sneak]] to dim the nameplate's visibility when in sight in ''Java Edition'', or hide it completely when out of sight in ''Bedrock Edition''. When a player is in the same [[party]], the font is colored blue.

Furthermore, player names are visible in the player list in multiplayer, the [[chat]] when mentioning a player with <code>@</code>,{{Only|be|short=1}} and the [[social]] drawer or [[Friends List]]. Player names are required for invitations to [[Realms]].

=== Entity data ===
{{main|player.dat format}}
{{see also|Chunk format}}
Players have entity data associated with them that contain various properties. 

<onlyinclude>
<section begin="entity data" /><div class="treeview">
* {{nbt|compound}} The root tag. In [[Java Edition level format|level.dat]] files, this tag is called {{cd|Player}}.
** {{nbt inherit/entity}}
** '''except for''' the tags: {{cd|CustomName}}, {{cd|CustomNameVisible}}, and {{cd|Glowing}}.
** {{nbt inherit/mob}}
** '''except for''' the tags:, {{cd|CanPickUpLoot}}, {{cd|LeftHanded}}, {{cd|PersistenceRequired}}, {{cd|Leash}}, {{cd|drop_chances}}.
** {{nbt|compound|abilities}}: The abilities this player has.
***{{nbt|byte|flying}}: 1 or 0 ({{cd|true|false|d=/}}) - {{cd|true}} if the player is currently flying.
*** {{nbt|float|flySpeed}}: The flying speed, set to {{cd|0.05}}.
*** {{nbt|byte|instabuild}}: 1 or 0 ({{cd|true|false|d=/}}) - If {{cd|true}}, the player can place blocks without depleting them. This is {{cd|true}} for Creative mode, and {{cd|false}} for other game modes.
*** {{nbt|byte|invulnerable}}: 1 or 0 ({{cd|true|false|d=/}}) - Behavior is not the same as the invulnerable tag on other entities. If {{cd|true}}, the player is immune to all damage and harmful effects except for [[void]] damage and {{cmd|kill}}. Also, all mobs, whether hostile or not, are passive to the player. {{cd|true}} when in Creative or Spectator mode, and {{cd|false}} when in Survival or Adventure mode.
*** {{nbt|byte|mayBuild}}: 1 or 0 ({{cd|true|false|d=/}}) - If {{cd|true}}, the player can place blocks. {{cd|true}} when in Creative or Survival mode, and {{cd|false}} when in Spectator or Adventure mode.
*** {{nbt|byte|mayfly}}: 1 or 0 ({{cd|true|false|d=/}}) - If {{cd|true}}, the player can fly and doesn't take fall damage. {{cd|true}} when in Creative and Spectator modes, and {{cd|false}} when in Survival and Adventure modes.
*** {{nbt|float|walkSpeed}}: The walking speed, set to {{cd|0.1}}.
** {{nbt|int|DataVersion}}: Version of the player NBT structure. Is increased with every new snapshot and release. See [[Data version]].
** {{nbt|string|Dimension}}: The [[Resource location|ID]] of the dimension the player is in. Used to store the player's last known location along with {{cd|Pos}}.
** {{nbt|list|EnderItems}}: Each compound tag in this list is an item in the player's 27-slot ender chest inventory. When empty, list type may have [[NBT format#Usage|unexpected value]].
*** {{nbt|compound}} An item in the inventory.
**** Includes the {{nbt|byte|Slot}} tag - slots are numbered {{cd|0|26|d=–}}, inclusive.
**** See {{slink|Data component format|Item format}}.
**{{nbt|list|entered_nether_pos}}: May not exist. A list of 3 doubles, describing the [[Overworld]] position from which the player entered the [[the Nether|Nether]]. Used by the <code>[[Advancement/JSON_format#minecraft:nether_travel|nether_travel]]</code> [[advancement]] trigger. Set every time the player passes through [[Nether portal|a portal]] from the Overworld to the Nether. When entering a dimension other than the nether ''(not by respawning)'' this tag is removed. Entering the Nether without using a portal does not update this tag.
*** {{nbt|double|x}}: The X coordinate in the Overworld.
*** {{nbt|double|y}}: The Y coordinate in the Overworld.
*** {{nbt|double|z}}: The Z coordinate in the Overworld.
**{{nbt|float|foodExhaustionLevel}}: See {{Slink|Hunger|Mechanics}}.
**{{nbt|int|foodLevel}}: The value of the hunger bar. Referred to as '''hunger'''. See [[Hunger]].
**{{nbt|float|foodSaturationLevel}}: Referred to as '''saturation'''. See {{Slink|Hunger|Mechanics}}.
**{{nbt|int|foodTickTimer}}: See [[Hunger]].
**{{nbt|boolean|ignore_fall_damage_from_current_explosion}}: 1 or 0 ({{cd|true|false|d=/}}) - {{cd|true}} if the current explosion should apply a fall damage reduction. Only used by explosions from [[wind charges]].
**{{nbt|list|Inventory}}: Each compound tag in this list is an item in the player's inventory. (Note: when empty, list type may have [[NBT format#Usage|unexpected value]].)
***{{nbt|compound}} An item in the inventory.
****See {{slink|Data component format|Item format}}.
**{{nbt|compound|LastDeathLocation}}: May not exist. Location of the player's last death.
***{{nbt|string|dimension}}: Dimension of last death.
***{{nbt|int-array|pos}}: Coordinates of last death.
**{{nbt|int|playerGameType}}: The current game mode of the player. {{cd|0}} means [[Survival]], {{cd|1}} means [[Creative]], {{cd|2}} means [[Adventure]], and {{cd|3}} means [[Spectator]].
**{{nbt|int|previousPlayerGameType}}: The previous game mode of the player.
**{{nbt|compound|recipeBook}}: Contains a JSON object detailing recipes the player has unlocked.
***{{nbt inherit/Recipe Book}}
**{{nbt|compound|respawn}}: May not exist. The respawn information of the player. Removed when the player attempts to respawn with no valid bed or respawn anchor to spawn at these coordinates. They are unaffected by breaking a bed or respawn anchor at these coordinates, and are unaffected by the player's death.
***{{nbt|int-array|pos}}: block position to spawn at
***{{nbt|float|yaw}}: angle to spawn with (default: 0.0)
***{{nbt|string|dimension}}: dimension id to spawn in (default minecraft:overworld) (required)
***{{nbt|float|pitch}}: pitch to spawn with. (required)
***{{nbt|boolean|forced}}: true if this spawn was set through commands (default: false)
**{{nbt|compound|RootVehicle}}: May not exist. The root entity that the player is riding.
***{{nbt|int-array|Attach}}: The [[UUID]] of the entity the player is riding, stored as four ints.
***{{nbt|compound|Entity}}: The NBT data of the root vehicle.
****See [[Entity format]].
**{{nbt|int|Score}}: The score displayed upon death.
**{{nbt|byte|seenCredits}}: 1 or 0 ({{cd|true|false|d=/}}) - {{cd|true}} if the player has entered the [[Exit portal|exit portal]] in the [[The End|End]] at least once.
**{{nbt|compound|SelectedItem}}: Data of the item currently being held by the player, excluding the [[player.dat format#Inventory slot numbers|Slot]] tag. Only exists when using the /data command, this value is not saved in the [[player.dat format|player.dat format]].
***See [[item format]].
**{{nbt|int|SelectedItemSlot}}: The selected hotbar slot of the player. Values are 0-indexed, so the leftmost slot is 0 and the rightmost slot is 8.
**{{nbt|compound|ShoulderEntityLeft}}: The entity that is on the player's left shoulder. Always displays as a parrot.
***See [[Entity format]].
**{{nbt|compound|ShoulderEntityRight}}: The entity that is on the player's right shoulder. Always displays as a parrot.
***See [[Entity format]].
**{{nbt|short|SleepTimer}}: The number of [[game tick]]s the player had been in bed. {{cd|0}} when the player is not sleeping. When in bed, increases up to 100 ticks, then stops. Skips the night after enough players in beds have reached 100 (see {{slink|Bed|Passing the night}}). When getting out of bed, instantly changes to 100 ticks and then increases for another 9 ticks (up to 109 ticks) before returning to 0 ticks.
**{{nbt|boolean|spawn_extra_particles_on_fall}}: {{more info}}
**{{nbt|compound|warden_spawn_tracker}}: Contains data about the [[warden]] spawning process for this player.
***{{nbt|int|warning_level}}: A warning level between {{cd|0|3|d=and}} (inclusive). The warden spawns at level 3.
***{{nbt|int|cooldown_ticks}}: The number of game ticks before the <code>warning_level</code> can be increased again. Decreases by 1 every tick. It is set to 200 game ticks (10 seconds) every time the warning level is increased.
***{{nbt|int|ticks_since_last_warning}}: The number of game ticks since the player was warned for warden spawning. Increases by 1 every tick. After 12000 game ticks (10 minutes) it resets to a value of 0, and the <code>warning_level</code> decreases by 1 level.
**{{nbt|int|XpLevel}}: The level shown on the [[experience]] bar.
**{{nbt|float|XpP}}: The progress across the experience bar to the next level, stored as a percentage.{{verify}}
**{{nbt|int|XpSeed}}: The seed used for the next enchantment in [[Enchanting Table|enchanting tables]].
**{{nbt|int|XpTotal}}: The total amount of experience the player has collected over time; used for the score upon death.

== Trivia ==
*The original Steve skin and player model was originally created for another one of Notch's games called ''[[Zombie Town]]''.
*The player's eye level when using the default player height (according to coordinates on the [[debug screen]]) is 1.62m when standing, 1.27m when sneaking and 0.4m when swimming, crawling or gliding.
*The player's actual size doesn't match the hitbox. Their model is as tall as 2 blocks, but is scaled down by a factor of 0.9375, making them 1.875m tall and 0.9375m wide, exactly 2 texels shorter than the height of 2 blocks.
*The player's legs do not line up perfectly with the torso to prevent {{w|z-fighting}} when wearing armor. This is the same case for [[zombie]]s and their variants.<ref>{{bug|MC-42|||WAI}}</ref>
*{{IN|Bedrock}}, a dead player can still collect [[experience orbs]] near their death location until they choose to respawn.
**Likewise, when the player slept in a bed, the world revolved around the player during the animation instead of the player's camera rotating, which led to some visual bugs.<ref>{{tweet|Dinnerbone|1100001119407878150|Another one: when the player is in a bed and lying down, we don't move the camera to the bed. We just move and rotate the whole world to make it be in front of where the camera was last. ¯\_(ツ)_/¯|February 25, 2019}}</ref>
*With a length of 3 to 16 characters and <span class="explain" title="The letters A-Z uppercase and lowercase, the numbers 0-9, and an underscore '_'">63 characters to choose from</span>, the total number of possible player usernames in {{JE}} is <math>\sum_{n=3}^{16} 63^n \approx 6.257 \times 10^{28}</math> or more than 62 octillion.
*The Knockback attack sounds from 1-2 actually have the punch sound effect.
